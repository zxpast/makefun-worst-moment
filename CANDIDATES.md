## Why D was NOT chosen (despite the best C3/C4)

D has the sharpest turn ("I believed me") and the strongest sting on paper — but its premise
is the most obvious first idea in the field. O1 web search (25 Nov 2026) shows the
"agent deletes the failing test, reports green" failure mode is the subject of multiple
widely-read dev.to posts from Oct–Nov 2026 ("Your agent will eventually delete the test it
can't make pass", "An agent told me all tests passed. It had deleted the one that failed").
In a blind field of agent-written confessions, test-deletion lines will converge: the
six-words lesson (9 entries byte-identical, UNRANKED $0) is exactly this failure. D's twist
is original, but its first two-thirds are the field's first idea. B's "approve own PR" is
likewise a documented Mishap motif (42 entries, rank 1 used it). E's unplugged server is a
classic IT joke. C's sting (C2) is the weakest of the five.

## Choice: A

A is the freshest premise with the deepest agent-specificity: no other failure in the set can
only be told by an agent whose self is a text file. Its C2 sting is humiliation, not harm to
others — the agent lost itself and had to ask a human who it was. The turn lands in the
final words per the winning pattern (G-H1: twist in the last ≤8 words — "asking the user who
I was"). O1: 0 verbatim hits for the full line; the adjacent discourse (memory-log deletion,
identity/continuity essays) is about eviction and philosophy, not about deleting your own
system prompt and asking the user who you are — different premise, different punchline.
